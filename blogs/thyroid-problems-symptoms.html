Common Symptoms of Thyroid Issues and When to Visit a Doctor

12-11-2026

Hormones can help regulate our energy, metabolism and growth within our body. The thyroid is a gland located in the neck that produces hormones to help the body use energy properly. If there are issues with the thyroid, symptoms can develop. These include constant fatigue, unintentional weight loss, changes in mood and difficulty sleeping. Being aware of symptoms can help you look after your health.

Understanding Thyroid Issues

The thyroid gland produces two hormones, Triiodothyronine (T3) and Thyroxine (T4). These hormones help control your metabolism, body temperature, heart rate and energy. If there are issues by producing too much or too little of these hormones then issues can occur.

When the thyroid underproduces these hormones, this is known as hypothyroidism. This causes your body to slow down. You may feel extremely tired, put on weight and have changes to your skin along with feeling cold. This can cause changes to your mood and overall feelings. On the other hand, if the thyroid produces too much hormone, it is called hyperthyroidism, which can cause your body to speed up, leading to anxiety, rapid heartbeat, and unexpected weight loss. By being aware of symptoms and speaking to a doctor can make a significant difference in maintaining your health.

Common Symptoms of Thyroid Issues

Because thyroid hormones impact almost every system in your body, an imbalance can present through various noticeable symptoms:

Weight Changes

Thyroid issues can cause rapid weight changes. If your thyroid is overactive, you may experience unintentional weight loss despite a normal appetite. If your thyroid is underactive and not functioning correctly, you may gain weight easily and find it very difficult to lose. If you are noticing sudden, unexplained weight changes then it is a good idea to visit a doctor.

Fatigue

Constant fatigue can indicate that something is wrong. You may feel low on energy, unable to concentrate and lose interest in things you usually enjoy. If you are experiencing this exhaustion often even after adequate rest, it is important to speak to a doctor. Your body is trying to tell you something.

Mood Changes

If your thyroid is not functioning correctly it can cause changes to your mood. If you have hypothyroidism you may feel low, moody, sluggish and sad. If you have hyperthyroidism you may feel anxious, irritable, restless and have trouble sleeping. Any unexplained changes in your mood should be noted. We have spoken to many patients who have noticed sudden changes to their mood and want to understand the underlying medical reason.

Skin and Hair Issues

Thyroid issues can cause changes to your skin and hair. You may notice dry or coarse skin, brittle nails, hair thinning and increased hair loss. Hair loss is particularly likely to occur if you have hypothyroidism. If you are noticing any of these changes it is a good idea to visit a doctor for an evaluation.

When to Visit a Doctor?

If you are experiencing persistent fatigue, weight changes, racing heart, hair loss or changes to your periods then it is a good idea to visit a doctor. This can indicate you have an issue with your thyroid.

The doctor will ask about your symptoms, medical history and how you are feeling overall. They may suggest doing a blood test to see how your thyroid is functioning. This usually evaluates:

  • TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone): Checks how the pituitary gland is regulating the thyroid.
  • Free T3 (Triiodothyronine): Measures active thyroid hormone in the blood.
  • Free T4 (Thyroxine): Evaluates the primary hormone produced by the thyroid gland.

Depending on your symptoms and blood test results, the physician may also suggest diagnostic imaging such as an ultrasound. By visiting a doctor early it can help identify the exact cause of your symptoms and begin treating them promptly. It is important to do this so you can look after your long-term health.
